About MicroCount STEVision

Empowering Student Researchers Through Computational Image Analysis

📖 Research Overview

MicroCount (STEVision) is a web-based computational system designed to detect, count, and assess suspected microplastic particles from microscope images of drinking water samples. The platform was developed to support environmental monitoring, student research, and scientific interpretation of water quality conditions in selected school settings.

🎯 Research Objectives

✔ Detect suspected microplastic particles from microscope images.
✔ Estimate particle counts using Fiji/ImageJ-assisted image processing.
✔ Classify contamination into different risk levels.
✔ Compare particle counts across selected school drinking water sources.
✔ Generate organized research reports and educational insights.

📘 About the Study

Seeing the Invisible: A Computational Analysis of Microplastics in School Drinking Water in Dasmariñas, Cavite

This study was conducted under the Special Program in Science, Technology and Engineering (STE) at Dasmariñas East Integrated High School and focuses on school drinking water sources in the city.

🧪 Methodology

The study uses a quantitative descriptive-comparative design to determine the presence and quantity of suspected microplastic particles in drinking water samples. Water samples are collected from school sources, examined under a microscope, and analyzed with Fiji/ImageJ and MicroCount (STEVision).

🤝 Acknowledgements

This website was developed to support student researchers conducting environmental studies involving drinking water quality and computational image analysis. We acknowledge the guidance of our research mentors, teachers, and school community in supporting this work.

⚠ Important Disclaimer

The risk assessment presented by MicroCount is an interpretative classification adapted from Li & Xing (2025). The website analyzes suspected microplastic particle counts obtained through computational image processing and does not directly measure microplastic concentration in μm/mL.

Results should be treated as preliminary screening data and may require laboratory verification for definitive contamination assessment.
